## Prespecified question

Across the prespecified sample of oncology RCT meta-analyses, how often does the secondary Egger asymmetry test flag a small-study association, and how often does the primary, prespecified PET-PEESE adjustment change statistical significance or the direction of the pooled estimate? Apply the adjustment to every evaluable synthesis, regardless of whether Egger flags asymmetry.

## Sampling and eligibility

This is a meta-research pilot across journals, not a sample restricted to the target journal. The database frame is Europe PMC, English-language records marked OPEN_ACCESS:Y, with first publication from 2017-01-01 through 2025-12-31 inclusive. These are complete calendar years within ten years of the planned 2026-10-27 search. No significance, effect magnitude, heterogeneity, citation, journal prestige, or harness availability filter is permitted. General biomedical journals are included.

Include published, human, conventional pairwise meta-analyses of randomized treatment comparisons, with **10 <= k <= 60 independent trials in the single designated synthesis**. The protocol's example of five trials does not override its explicit minimum of ten for these methods. Require a freely readable full text, operationalised as an open-access full text retrievable from Europe PMC (fullTextXML, with figures and supplements via its supplementaryFiles service) without payment, login or circumvention of access controls. Each trial, outcome, contrast, follow-up, effect scale, and original pooling model must be identifiable. No digitization of bar widths, Kaplan-Meier reconstruction, borrowing missing outcomes, or fetching new trial-level datasets. Require the original pooled estimate and CI. Accept RR, OR, HR on the log scale and MD or SMD on their original analysis scales. Require positive finite sampling variances.

For this pilot, restrict the designated original pooling method to conventional inverse-variance fixed effect or inverse-variance random effects using DL or REML, with identifiable Wald or Hartung-Knapp inference and any variance modification explicitly documented. Multi-arm trials qualify only when the designated synthesis has one independent contrast per trial, using the original documented arm combination. Independent reviews of overlapping trials remain separate; identify overlap later and do not imply independent participant totals.

## Fixed tests, thresholds and adjustment

Let y_i be the independent study estimate on its analysis scale and s_i > 0 its standard error, v_i = s_i^2. Ratios use natural logs; no mixing of effect metrics within a synthesis. Preserve the original intervention-versus-comparator orientation and record which direction favours the intervention. Null = 0 on this scale.

**Primary adjustment: conditional PET-PEESE**, applied to all gate-passing syntheses:
1. Fit PET weighted least squares: y_i = b0 + b1*s_i + error_i, with weights 1/v_i, an intercept and no other moderators. Use residual multiplicative dispersion phi = sum(w_i * residual_i^2)/(k-2); covariance = phi * inverse(X'WX). Do not add tau-squared weights or clamp phi to one. Use a t distribution with k-2 degrees of freedom for coefficient tests and two-sided 95% intervals.
2. Test PET's b0 against zero, two-sided alpha = 0.05. If p < 0.05, use PEESE; otherwise use PET, including equality at 0.05.
3. PEESE fits y_i = c0 + c1*v_i + error_i with weights 1/v_i, its own residual dispersion, the same degrees of freedom, no other moderators and a two-sided 95% t interval. The final adjusted estimate is c0 and its interval when step 2 selects PEESE, or b0 and its interval otherwise.
4. Report both intercepts, PET branch-selection p, the selected branch and its final interval. Zero residual dispersion, singularity, nonfinite results or unresolved numerical instability are method failures, not evidence of perfect precision. Do not swap to trim-and-fill, limit meta-analysis or an alternative estimator after failure.

**Secondary asymmetry test: classical Egger.** In the PET regression above, test the SE coefficient b1 = 0 using the two-sided t test with k-2 df; flag at p < 0.10. The flag is applied to the raw, unrounded two-sided p-value exported by the app and computed independently in R (metafor `regtest(..., model="lm", predictor="sei")`), not to the app's displayed verdict label. That label marks "evidence of bias" at p < 0.05 (pubbias-tests/index.html at allmeta 14dc545b) and is ignored for this pilot. App and R p-values must agree within the gate-3 tolerance before the flag is set. This coefficient corresponds to the intercept in the standardized-normal-deviate formulation. The PET b0 test is a different test, used only for the primary adjustment branch. Keep these p-values separate. No Begg, Harbord, Peters, trim-and-fill, or alternative adjustment is confirmatory in this pilot.

Implementation reference: the package author's [metafor regtest documentation](https://wviechtb.github.io/metafor/reference/regtest.html), accessed 2026-10-27, describes classical Egger via model="lm", predictor="sei" and the conditional PET-PEESE procedure. The equations and thresholds above are the prespecified design choices. Verify the app's actual implementation against them before freezing its version.

**Change definitions:** unadjusted reference = the original-model estimate and 95% interval that passed reproduction. Significance = the unrounded interval strictly excludes zero (one on ratio display scale). A significance change is either significant to nonsignificant or nonsignificant to significant. A direction reversal requires original and adjusted point estimates to have strictly opposite signs. An exact zero is a separate neutral category, not a reversal. Primary changed-conclusion indicator = significance change OR strict direction reversal. Count a synthesis once in that union; also report the components and their overlap. A CI endpoint exactly at zero is nonsignificant; unresolved rounding near zero is indeterminate. No rule involving the apparent desirability of a change is permitted.

A fixed sensitivity comparison uses the same PET-PEESE result against a conventional inverse-variance random-effects REML/Wald unadjusted baseline on the same trials. This isolates dependence on the original baseline model; it does not replace the primary comparison. Report this separately, even when it disagrees. Other sensitivity analyses require a prospectively documented amendment before extraction.

Small-study associations are not proof of publication bias: heterogeneity, effect/SE coupling (especially SMDs or binary outcomes), and sparse data can also matter. PET-PEESE is a model-based sensitivity estimate, not an identified unbiased effect. Report results by RR/OR/HR/MD/SMD category descriptively; do not make clinical recommendations. No multiplicity correction is applied to the per-synthesis descriptive flags, and no global test of publication bias is claimed.

## Mandatory gates and reuse

All steps in this section are future work, not work performed for this draft.

1. **AI double extraction with adjudication.** Extraction is performed entirely by AI models; no person extracts data. Two models extract independently from the same source version (the version of record PDF; if the Europe PMC rendering of that same version is used, record which), without seeing each other's output: Codex (OpenAI) and agy running Claude (Anthropic) or Gemini (Google), with the model name and version recorded. Compare all trial identities, contrasts, outcomes, time points, effect scales, arm sizes, numerical inputs, model settings and any chronology fields. Report initial exact field-level agreement and the number of discrepant fields. A third model, from a family used by neither extractor (Gemini if agy ran Claude; Claude if agy ran Gemini), adjudicates every discrepant field against the source PDF. It sees the two candidate values unlabelled as to which extractor produced them, and must cite the page and figure/table for its value. It may return a third value only with such a citation. Final values must be complete for every required field at source precision. An unresolved required field excludes the review. Agreement percentages never excuse a material unresolved discrepancy. The reproduction gate (2) is the main correctness check on the extracted set.
2. **Original-model reproduction.** Reproduce the original designated pooled point estimate AND both 95% CI limits using its own documented model, tau-squared estimator, CI method, corrections and handling of trial arms. On the published scale, each value must differ by no more than half the last displayed decimal unit: tolerance = 0.5 * 10^(-d) + 1e-12 for a number printed to d decimal places (equivalent last-place tolerance for scientific notation). For ratios compare exponentiated results. Do not use a percentage tolerance or widen it after failure. If higher-precision source data exist, use them. Require agreement of significance/direction; rounding that leaves a boundary unresolved is indeterminate, not a pass. Identify a transcription or documented implementation error once through adjudication, then rerun the same specification. If reproduction still fails, exclude and report the reason and discrepancies. Do not tune the estimator to force agreement.
3. **App/R agreement.** Only after gates 1-2 may the new method run in the app and independently in R using the package employed in that app paper's validation. Pinned implementation (checked 2026-10-27): app = allmeta `pubbias-tests/` at commit `14dc545bcba87a02158b2f445c28450e7eaf6b89` (last change to the app f7aae4f3). ## Outputs, denominators, and new versus robust

Primary headline: C/M adjustment-evaluable meta-analyses changed significance or direction, with the union definition above. Secondary headline: E/T with an Egger p < 0.10 among test-evaluable meta-analyses. Also report both counts against the selected denominator S, all exclusion reasons and unresolved counts. Show significant-to-nonsignificant, nonsignificant-to-significant, strict direction reversals, overlap of these events and unchanged conclusions. Report a 2x2 table of Egger flag versus adjusted conclusion change among jointly evaluable reviews, without conditioning the primary denominator on an Egger flag. When a denominator is zero, report not estimable.

One summary figure: stacked paired categories for original versus adjusted significance/direction, with raw denominators and missing/failure categories. Extended data/archive: every selected review, DOI, outcome, k, metric, original model and estimate/CI, reproduction differences/tolerances, extraction agreement/adjudication, Egger coefficient/p/flag, PET intercept/p, PEESE intercept, chosen branch, adjusted estimate/CI, separate change flags, sensitivity result, novelty/provenance, and exclusion/implementation failures. Do not pool unrelated clinical effect magnitudes. Report descriptive proportions without treating overlapping reviews as independent samples for inferential field-level confidence intervals.

Final journal narrative: 150-250 words plus one summary figure; every individual result in Extended data and the archive. Headline template: "In C of M reproducible oncology meta-analyses, PET-PEESE changed significance or effect direction; Egger flagged asymmetry in E of T. These are sensitivity findings in the registered sample." No numbers are filled in now.

"New" = previously unreported PET-PEESE result or field-level frequency under the fixed frame; an adjustment-driven change is a finding, not a prerequisite for inclusion or publication. "Robust" = neither significance nor strict direction changes under the primary adjustment; state explicitly if the fixed sensitivity comparison differs. Egger-negative is not proof of absence of bias. Failures/indeterminate cases are neither robust nor changed.

  1. Codex views the designated synthesis' forest plot, retrieved via the Europe PMC supplementaryFiles service. It transcribes **only the row labels** of the trials contributing to the designated pooled result, plus any printed arm, dose or shared-control annotation. It transcribes no numbers.
  2. A deterministic check (`supporting/second_pass.py`) normalises each label: lower-case; drop punctuation; drop trailing a/b/c suffixes and arm, dose or group descriptors. Independence is **met** only if every normalised label is unique and no row is annotated as a shared or split control or a separate arm of the same trial. Independence is **not met** if any normalised label repeats or any such annotation is present. It **stays unresolved** if the plot cannot be read, or the designated rows cannot be identified. *Clarification added 2026-10-28 while the pass was running, in the strict direction and applied to every record:* independence counts as met only when one label was transcribed per designated trial (number of labels = k_designated). A partial row list cannot show that no trial repeats. The trigger was one record (P14 rank 506), where 8 labels were transcribed for k = 12; it stays unresolved.
  3. A record whose independence is met is re-decided by the same `decide.py`, including the stage-D per-study and model checks. Every other criterion is unchanged.
  All other UNRESOLVED records, and any record the pass cannot settle, remain not eligible. Seeded order and the 30-review cap are unchanged. For P06, a record ranked below 3265 that becomes eligible enters the seeded order and displaces the last selected review. This is a pre-specified procedure, not a loosening: a repeated label still excludes, including when two distinct trials happen to share an author-year label.
- **2026-10-28, AM-H2 (investigator design change, Mahmood; before release): capped human verification.** This replaces AM-H1's human scope. Old scope: all discrepant fields, all rows of reviews passing after adjudication, a 10% sample, and headline reviews; escalation to a full human check. New scope: humans check every row of headline reviews and a 5% sample, at least 1 row per review. Adjudicated discrepancies are re-checked by a fourth AI pass, and only fields still in dispute reach a human. Escalation becomes a full AI re-extraction plus a human check of headline rows. Packets are capped at about 30 minutes and one per co-author per week, and an unreturned packet is reassigned after 7 days. Reason: to keep co-author load bounded. Made before any extraction and without reference to results.

## Freeze and future release

Before any future trial extraction: complete the full database search and eligibility/novelty log; retain the specified rules unless a dated amendment is needed; pin the app and validation-package versions; freeze the seed, frame and method document; record its SHA-256 and UTC time; and follow the protocol's GitHub release plus Zenodo DOI procedure. The server-side release must exist before extraction, and its Zenodo archival DOI is to be recorded. No release or deposition is made in this draft-only task. Registration changes must precede extraction and explain the change; feasibility limitations do not authorize outcome switching. Preserve the paper's existing use case.

The final report must state all attrition, gate failures, missingness and method limitations, report whatever result occurs, and make no clinical recommendations or priority/ranking claims.
